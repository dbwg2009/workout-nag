Key Insights

Body composition

You're already lean at 12.9% body fat. You don't need to lose fat — you need to build muscle. Definition comes from muscle under the skin, not from cutting. Eat enough.

Strength imbalance

7+ pull-ups but only 1–5 press-ups. Your rowing background put your back & biceps ahead. Your chest & triceps are the priority (trained 3×/week) — expect fast progress. Friday adds a 2nd back session so pulling doesn't stall.

Train smart, not to failure

On most sets, stop with 1–2 reps left in the tank (reps in reserve). Only push the last set of an exercise close to true failure. This builds nearly the same muscle with far less fatigue and lower injury risk — the evidence is clear on this.

Sleep — your weak point

6–7 hrs is limiting muscle repair. Getting to 7.5 hrs consistently is the single highest-leverage change outside training. Muscle is built asleep, not in the session.

Exam season — stay flexible

You're sitting GCSEs. If a day is too much, the Friday session is the first to drop — it's a short bonus, not essential. Consistency over 8 weeks beats a perfect week followed by burnout. Missing a session never undoes your progress.

How to read the sets

e.g. "3 × 10–12 (1–2 RIR)" = 3 sets of 10–12 reps, stopping each set with 1–2 reps in reserve. "× max" = as many clean reps as you can with good form (still stop if form breaks). Rest 60–90 sec between sets.

Rowing — Once a Week

Your rowing background is why your pull-ups are already strong. Once a week, do a 20–30 min easy row if you have erg access. This is active recovery, not training — keep it easy. It maintains your cardio base and that muscle memory. No erg? A 20 min brisk walk does the same job.

Fuelling the Build

Nutrition

No calorie counting. No meal plans. Three targeted changes only. Target: ~120–130g protein per day (≈2g per kg — right in the evidence-backed range for muscle growth).

The Next Progressions

Option A — Stay Bodyweight, Go Harder

Progress to harder variations: archer & pseudo-planche press-ups, one-arm negatives, pistol squats, L-sits, toes-to-bar. Serious functional strength with zero equipment cost.

Option B — Add the Gym (Now Worth It)

After 8 weeks you'll have a real pressing base. Now bench press, cable flyes and dumbbell work will add mass faster than bodyweight alone. At this point £3/visit is genuinely worth it and you'll know what you're doing.

Option C — Resistance Bands (£15–20 one-off)

Bands bridge bodyweight and gym — banded press-ups, rows, curls, tricep extensions, and leg work at home. A full set costs less than a month of gym visits and lasts years.
